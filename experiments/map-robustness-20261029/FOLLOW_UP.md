# Evidence-based follow-up

1. **History correctness:** preserve the timestamp-validity and death-reset repair as an isolated change (PR #50). It converts two baseline losses and loses one baseline win in 560 matched cases; gem margin is essentially unchanged. Validate with stronger opponent snapshots and fresh seeds before treating it as a competitive upgrade. Keep the signed healing feature unchanged so the learned model's input semantics remain stable.
2. **Carrier routing and fuel:** reject the tested 16-round-refresh implementation. It loses eight net wins, including two on the_pack, and has 30 fuel-exhaustion events versus baseline's 23. Inspect the paired cases before another implementation. A future version should budget path expansion and invalidate routes from meaningful carrier/path changes, rather than blindly increasing refresh frequency. Acceptance: no broad paired regression, no higher fuel-exhaustion rate, and an improvement across withheld map families/strong opponents. Public pathfinding work offers design ideas, not a tested current-season implementation.
3. **Danger banking and combination:** retain as experiments. The policy alone converts 15 losses but loses 16 wins; the combination converts 20 and loses 21. A slightly better mean gem margin does not override those tradeoffs. Inspect interrupted banking/route decisions in both gained and lost cases; do not add exact-map threshold overrides to rescue a weak global policy.
4. **Opponent difficulty:** keep the three archived bots as cheap regression sentinels, then obtain current-season opponent snapshots from distinct strategies. Keep their sources fixed during tuning and hold out opponent families as well as layouts. The 98.8% win rate against old opponents is too saturated to guide fine selection, given the 10% win rate in the separate current top-ten sample. Opponent selection and patch/version differences prevent pooling those rates.
5. **Map review:** start with Collision, which loses in multiple historical cohorts, and current top-ten Quadrants/Expanse/Rows. Review matched win and loss replays for first-bank delay, time carrying gems, travel per deposit, contested-camp commitment and fuel interruption. These are diagnostics to measure; the outcome table alone does not establish their cause. Compare under the same opponent, version and patch with both sides before attributing weakness to topology. The archived v24z/v25a/v25c cohorts all losing every game indicate broad strength issues, not a map-specific diagnosis.
6. **Generalisation discipline:** keep map byte hashes and procedural/topological features, not display names, as identity and adaptation inputs. Cover all 70 current layouts, reserve new layouts before tuning, keep seed sets disjoint, and report worse-map tails alongside aggregate wins. Capture training-map/opponent/engine provenance for the frozen learned coefficients. Retain the learned adjustments: the learned-off diagnostic loses 29 net wins across 560 cases, including 20 on old layouts and nine on added layouts. This is evidence against the removal, not proof the model is free of overfitting; the test is exploratory and also changes compute cost.
7. **History completeness:** obtain a complete team match export with match/group IDs, map hash, outcome, bot version, opponent, side and engine patch. Deduplicate IDs and separate ranked/unranked cohorts. The supplied 313-ID report is useful but incomplete; profile lifetime totals are not a substitute for individual map outcomes.
8. **Future map capacity:** shared target packing has a six-bit group constraint. No sampled current baseline event established a captured group ID >=63, so this is not a claimed cause of current losses. Before larger-map support, define a safe representation and legal fallback and test boundary IDs 62/63/64 against the actual SDK group enumeration.
