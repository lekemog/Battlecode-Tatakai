# Initial ranked-match study: top-50 cohort, first sample

17 October 2026. Analysis only; no bot changed or submitted.

## Scope and evidence

This is **not a census of all 50 teams**. The first sample contains 25 completed ranked games across 14 maps, from five consecutive series involving Intercontinental Fish. Both teams in every pairing were in the top seven in the observed ladder snapshot (about 19:41 UK/Irish time). Six distinct teams appear. Twelve authenticated JSON summaries were successfully downloaded and parsed, producing 24 team-game feature records. Remaining games have overview evidence only. Exports were rate-limited; no blocked download is counted as a summary.

Ranks at snapshot: Fish 1, Mite 2, Sneaky Bush Campers 3, Nyxius 4, procless 5, AlphaSlop 7. Historical opponents and bot versions may differ from current ranks. All games involve Fish, so no result below estimates a population-wide top-50 win rate or causal effect.

[evidence.json](top50-evidence-2026-10-17.json) records all 25 overview results, the 24 detailed team rows, summary SHA-256 checksums, source series identifiers and metric definitions. Re-download using the series Summary → Download JSON, or the official CLI with individual match IDs below. No private debug logs, credentials or session data are included.

## What the summaries actually show

### 1. Preserve gems and convert economy; do not optimise death count alone

In elkring, [Fish vs procless](https://2026a.battlecode.co.uk/game/match-groups/80f7be78-d289-4c6d-a912-3b5c2daf95a5), game 1, match `957095d1-cbfa-4cfc-bca3-e29e62ad186e`: Fish wins 1008–612. Camp-clear events are tied 24–24 and earned gold is relatively close, 5035–4830, but deaths are 4–13 and gem-loss event sums 108–288. This supports investigating conversion and exposure, rather than just the number of camps cleared.

Counterexample in the same series, game 2, ambush, match `ef0d96e4-7fd7-4985-8f02-48b36df9566a`: both sides have zero deaths and zero gem losses, yet procless wins 1022–668 in 501 rounds. Earned gold is 3630 vs 3020. Death avoidance is not enough; safe farming throughput and banking still matter.

**Experiment:** log carried gems when choosing the next camp, altar or recall, and the travel/channel cost of each alternative. Test one risk-aware banking change separately from a farm-routing change. Accept on wins and submitted gems, with deaths and lost gems as explanatory metrics, not the optimisation target. Gem-loss totals can count recirculating stolen gems, so do not treat them as unique preventable score.

### 2. Contest needs a distinct objective plan, but 100% passive farming is not established

Two detailed contest games:
- [Sneaky vs Fish](https://2026a.battlecode.co.uk/game/match-groups/8a4560dd-bbcb-4570-ada3-7b85eafe39bc), game 1, match `041dbc5a-73e7-4d88-80af-0c4dd0a1bea8`: Fish wins 1000–0 at round 1752; Fish/Sneaky earned gold 10773/3340, camp clears 80/20, deaths 29/61.
- [Fish vs AlphaSlop](https://2026a.battlecode.co.uk/game/match-groups/58cf350e-df32-4cee-8aa9-4c39b219ae52), game 1, match `76a82dc6-96d6-4915-a0c0-5300ed607909`: Fish wins 1000–0 at round 1582; Fish/Alpha earned gold 9492/4807, camp clears 63/40, deaths 30/73.

Fish uses Stellar, Tavi, Prism, Cassi & Issac, Ren in both. Its first full emblem differs: Bruiser Health at round 135 against Sneaky, Treasure Focus at 119 against AlphaSlop. This does **not** overturn our own rejected Treasure-first experiment; different champions, item owners and opponents confound the comparison. Runtime rosters list Issac separately: six runtime entities are five draft picks.

**Inference:** economy, survival and coordinated objective conversion all merit investigation. These summaries cannot establish exact movement routes, pack formation, siege timing or whether crowd control caused the wins. Fish still suffers 29–30 deaths, so “avoid all combat” is not the demonstrated strategy.

**Experiment:** choose one change after W9: reduce exposed solo farming OR introduce a coordinated monolith commitment criterion. Keep draft/items fixed initially. Record gold and alive combat power at commitment, deaths before the objective, monolith capture and successful banking. Then test crowd-control draft independently.

### 3. High ward counts and locks are tools, not a strategy by themselves

[Fish vs Mite](https://2026a.battlecode.co.uk/game/match-groups/7094b7fb-5716-4a8a-a405-e64e05a0bef3), swirl: Mite places 219 wards and buys four locks, but loses 294–1116 to Fish's 39 wards and zero locks. First purchases are round 107 vs 42; earned gold 2305 vs 6395; camp clears 7 vs 22. Ward volume is not evidence of good decisions.

Contest counterexamples are stronger: Sneaky places 417 wards vs Fish 191 and loses; AlphaSlop places 387 vs Fish 126 and buys 15 locks but also loses. Do not target their placement count.

Conversely, AlphaSlop wins corners with eight locks and lanes with four, while Fish buys none in either. Locks are worth an isolated trial, but these are associations and do not show that the locks caused either win.

**Experiment:** verify current ward/lock API costs and cooldowns, then use a ward specifically to resolve a banking or contested-camp decision. Log the information gained and whether the chosen action changed. For locks, test a narrowly triggered banking policy with a gold cap; compare successful tributes and upgrade timing, not purchases alone.

### 4. First purchase timing must be interpreted per map and per champion

Across the 24 detailed team rows, median first team purchase is round 51.5; excluding four contest rows it is 50 (20 rows). This small convenience sample does not replace the earlier top-50 analysis.

In lanes vs Mite, Fish wins 1006–986 with its first purchase at 57 vs 62; neither loses any gems. In ambush vs procless, the winner's first purchase is **later** (83 vs 76). A global “buy by round 45” rule is not justified.

The losing eight-map Mite side first buys at 75, completes its first emblem at 249, loses 572 gems and dies 21 times; Fish buys at 38, loses 220 gems and dies seven times. Timing and snowball are entangled; purchase delay may follow weak income or death loops.

**Experiment:** measure first affordability, actual purchase and full-emblem completion separately for each champion. Trial an affordable first-item recall only where the expected lost farming/channel/travel time is smaller than the expected return from the upgrade. Hold the build order fixed. Compare fixed-horizon income and win outcomes.

### 5. Map specialisation and matchup variety belong in evaluation

Fish loses the AlphaSlop series 1–4 but wins contest; it wins the Sneaky and Nyxius series 4–1 but loses expanse to both. This suggests map-level diagnostics, not that AlphaSlop is universally strongest or expanse is universally bad for Fish.

The 22 overview games excluding contest and stampede finish in a median 495 rounds. Contest finishes at 1582/1752 and stampede at 1094. These are different timing regimes. Across the 25 games, five losing sides still reach at least 850 submitted gems. Banking and objective completion can matter in close races, but final scores do not reveal unbanked bags.

**Experiment:** report regressions separately for contest/stampede, death-heavy contested maps, wall-heavy routes and farming races. Use map and opponent paired comparisons, both sides where possible. Do not pool raw lifetime gold, ward counts or deaths across very different match lengths.

## Poker-style adaptation: how to make these observations useful

Use a small uncertain opponent model, not labels inferred from one loss. Distinguish farming pace, invasion evidence, willingness to chase low-value targets and altar pressure only from legal observations available in-match. Keep tactical danger separate from longer-lived style estimates (see Issue #1). The samples above show both uncontested economic losses and lethal snowballs; one “aggression” knob cannot be assumed to diagnose both.

Model interventions should be bounded by confidence and evaluated via ablations. Replays/summaries are offline research evidence, not omniscient runtime inputs. No cross-match runtime persistence is proposed.

## Data quality fixes for subsequent analysis

- Expand beyond this Fish-centred sample to ranks 11–25 and 26–50 and non-Fish pairings before claiming general top-50 patterns.
- Preserve team IDs, timestamped rank snapshot, series ID, individual match ID, map, side and bot/submission version if exposed. Deduplicate games shared by multiple team histories.
- In `tools/feat.py`, `n=len(champs)` counts Issac too; do not interpret it as pick count.
- `tools/cmp.py` counts the earliest three team-wide combination events; that is **not** a per-champion first-emblem distribution. The source script acknowledges missing ownership in its aggregation; retain event owner when calculating per-champion openings.
- Ward and consumable counts must be interpreted against duration, champion count and actual decision utility.
- Missing first tribute is censored/non-completion, not a row to silently drop from the comparison.
- Fixed-horizon metrics in evidence are null if the match has already ended; do not carry final values forward and compare them with still-running games.
- Existing scripts silently skip failures. Report attempted, successful and excluded summary counts with reasons.
- Do not call every non-camp gemCollection a kill without validating its context.
- Export limits were observed; wait the server's retry interval, retain successful files and stop batches on 429 rather than hammering requests.
