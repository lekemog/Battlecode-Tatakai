# Field research: public Cambridge Battlecode 2026 bots

Shared LionelMSISS research reference, reviewed **20 October 2026 UTC**. Suggestions are for team review and isolated tests.

## Scope and evidence

We screened **90 repository candidates** through name, description, README, season-keyword, fork-inclusive and October-creation searches. **29 match the autumn champion/gem ruleset**, including one duplicate and one evaluation framework. The remainder comprises 47 spring-game repositories/collections/experiments, two placeholders, ten unrelated/other-game results, and two empty repositories.

This corrects our earlier narrow search. Every candidate found in this sweep has an entry below. Search indexing is incomplete: this is **not an exhaustive census of the internet**. We reviewed representative source files and gave the most relevant current-game designs additional function-level review; we did not audit every line of every historical version. The [source manifest](FIELD_RESEARCH_SOURCES.json) records 189 retrieved source/document files, exact paths, content hashes, immutable Git blob URLs, review depth and exclusions. Retrieval coverage is broader than deep semantic review.

No matches were run for this report. Opponent gameplay source was not copied into Polaris; no submissions, platform scrims or deployments were made. Missing root licenses do not authorize redistribution. The manifest records root license filenames, not a full licensing audit.

## Most useful implementation findings

### 1. Price the whole camp-to-score cycle

**Observed:** dpark Daniel v7's `choose_target()` prices overlapping travel/respawn time, clearing, capture, altar travel, tribute and healing trips. It divides reward among assigned bodies, discounts extra helpers and bounds waiting/stalling. Source: `bots/daniel/v7/src/bot.cpp`, especially lines633–740 and753–818.

**Polaris proposal:** compare incremental team reward per occupied champion-round through completed delivery. Include gold's measured upgrade value, rather than a universal gold-to-gem constant. Score the entire connected group: leaving one member alive can prevent capture.

**Countercheck:** their ally DPS, group caps and survivability estimates are approximate. A claim is intent, not arrival. Our v21b solo-farming loss in #33 means independent farmers alone are not the solution.

### 2. Separate clearing, capture ownership and delivery

**Observed:** Mohnish Bot1 v4 elects/caches one visible capture recipient, releases helpers and re-elects after invalidation. Tarot Foundry also elects a capturer. Conversely, Tarot squad-v21 invites nearby capture sharing and reports a favorable local league result.

**Polaris proposal:** test one recipient with helpers reassigned against selective sharing when another body can reach a useful upgrade sooner. Measure team throughput and subsequent item timing. Use stable tie-breaking, expiry and a fallback for disagreement under limited vision.

**Countercheck:** concentrating rewards can starve other jobs; sharing can waste multiple bodies on a short wait. Neither is universally correct. An unseen ally is not necessarily dead, idle or abandoning a claim.

### 3. Precompute a practical opening, then repair it

**Observed:** Tarot Foundry compiles local cyclic routes/altar stops, with bounded route polishing and a runtime FSM. Apex encodes a body/squad economy plan in draft memory.

**Polaris proposal:** precompute static distances, camp contents, feasible kit assignments and the first two farm cycles. Repair assignments using observed pressure, camp changes, unavailable allies and blocked paths. Start with the opening rather than replacing the entire controller.

**Countercheck:** static routes can fail under contested ownership. Recheck draft fuel/memory assumptions in our official SDK, and keep hidden dynamic enemy state unknown.

### 4. Bound commitments and record actual progress

**Observed:** itz-lakshya uses a rescoring interval and switch margin. Tarot has route-failure and failed-sortie cooldowns. Photon centralizes and latches banking intent. Cope-Pasta's older interfaces distinguish failed, done and progress results.

**Polaris proposal:** retain purpose, target, owner, start round, last actual progress, completion and abandonment reason. Protect a safe nearly completed capture/tribute; release stale work after death or proven route failure. Failed attacks/moves must not falsely consume the policy chain.

**Countercheck:** stationary turns can be casts, captures or justified respawn waits. Generic stall counters must distinguish those from unproductive idling and fuel suspension.

### 5. Preserve resource memory across vision boundaries

**Observed:** userVatsal and Not-TNB retain seen respawn deadlines; itz-lakshya keeps camp HP and rebuilds blockers after dash-induced vision changes. These address moving away from a dead camp, forgetting it, then moving back.

**Polaris proposal:** store state, observation round and confidence separately. Schedule arrival using a known deadline; inspect uncertain unseen camps instead of assuming alive/dead/owned. Expire danger and claims independently.

**Countercheck:** an enemy may have changed the group since the observation. A remembered timer is an estimate.

### 6. Make siege positions and completion explicit

**Observed:** Mohnish siege positions intersect actual spell range with tiles outside every remaining monster's attack area. Its notes distinguish Chebyshev monster range and Euclidean spell range. Daniel v7 extends siege treatment to oversized elk groups.

**Polaris proposal:** assign eligible casters to legal firing tiles while others maintain income or protect the route. Recompute after monsters die, then transition explicitly from final kill to capture to delivery.

**Countercheck:** nominal range four is not a universally safe diagonal ring. Terrain, occupancy, enemy arrival and runtime range values determine feasibility. A caster draft alone does not create a siege plan.

### 7. Decide whether an escape actually breaks attacks

**Observed:** Not-TNB can strike after retreat movement, uses a short damage horizon and returns false on a blocked chase. Claude already owns v21a's fight-back trial.

**Polaris proposal:** classify escape feasibility using range, terrain, dash/defense availability and arrival times; choose legal combinations of movement, retaliation and protection. A safe early escape remains valuable.

**Countercheck:** equal speed alone does not prove escape impossible. “Always fight” would introduce different failures. Extend v21a's evidence rather than starting a duplicate trial.

### 8. Adopt measurement contracts

**Observed:** DeySag checks collected = deposited + lost + final carried gems and distinguishes linked-body deaths from kill rewards. micache preserves missing cases, faults, unknown reliability and holdouts in its evaluator.

**Polaris proposal:** use those accounting checks in replay audits. Record immutable source, SDK/engine, sides, seeds, exclusions and per-map paired changes. Separate fault, fuel delay, productive wait and tactical death.

**Countercheck:** opponent local results/postmortems are their evidence until reproduced. Starter sweeps establish a floor, not top100 strength.

## Competitive gaming ideas and our adaptation

The esports sources below offer general ideas, not current patch/item recommendations. These are our proposed transfers.

| Primary source / published idea | Polaris adaptation | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [Team Liquid TI13 analysis](https://teamliquid.com/articles/team-liquids-ti13-win-by-the-numbers): flexible farm priorities and roles around when teammates become effective | Give resources to the next useful power spike; others create space or protect delivery | Better team output, without starving another necessary job |
| [Dignitas interview with Biofrost](https://dignitas.gg/articles/a-comprehensive-support-guide-with-dignitas-biofrost): engaging, disengaging, pressure and zoning are support jobs | Escort protects interception routes and preserves useful defensive abilities | Extra gems delivered exceed escort's lost farming |
| [Dignitas macro guide](https://dignitas.gg/articles/macro-game-level-up-a-mid-to-late-game-league-of-legends-guide): prepare position before an objective | Require usable HP/abilities, coordinated arrival, completion time and an exit | Fewer staggered entries/abandoned objectives without excessive waiting |
| [Dignitas recall guide](https://dignitas.gg/articles/going-home-how-to-time-recalls-to-create-an-advantage): compare a useful reset with the opportunity surrendered | Price the actual upgrade/heal against travel and the imminent capture window | More productive complete cycles; stagger recalls when appropriate |

After an enemy retreats or dies, ask **what can we safely convert before they return?** Finish a capture, bank, take a camp or prepare the next approach. Chasing beyond that window can surrender the advantage. When behind, productive safe resources can fund the next feasible contest.

Opponent modelling should estimate response delays, chasing persistence, repeated farm routes, altar guarding and reset timing. Maintain evidence counts, uncertainty and recency using legal observations. For unseen enemies, model reachable threat areas rather than exact positions. Change one decision at a time; a broad aggression label that turns the whole team defensive can concede the only scoring route. Judge the model by improved decisions/outcomes, not classification accuracy alone.

## Independent test queue for Claude's review

Use the accepted live baseline at test time, frozen sources and the established external panel. These are designs for review.

| Priority | Experiment | First maps | Mechanism to verify | Acceptance / countercheck |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete-cycle opening jobs | corners, rushbait_v2, packs; ambush control | Candidate rejection reasons, travel, clear, capture, delivery and recipient | Better early useful income and score; preserve ambush/stampede |
| 2 | Capture owner with prompt helper release | rows, corners, packs | Recipient changes and helper's next productive action | More team reward per body-round; compare selective sharing |
| 3 | Respawn memory plus bounded commitment | lanes, rows, interlock | Vision-edge loops, switches, justified waits and reinspections | Less wasted travel without abandoning captures or blocking respawns |
| 4 | Runtime opening-plan repair | ordinary farm maps | After observed contest/death, repaired work actually executes | Gain survives enemy-held camps and withheld layouts |
| 5 | Escort as route protection | eight, collision | Available escort covers the dangerous segment; arrival gaps logged | Delivery gain exceeds lost farming; no permanent full-team convoy |
| 6 | Ready siege and finish transition | monolith, elkring; stampede control | Firing legality, budget, final-kill→capture→tribute | More score conversion, not only damage |
| 7 | Escape/retaliation sequencing | contest, eight | Extend v21a evidence: legal counterfire, defense and separation gained | Less death/downtime; no endless fights or canceled safe escapes |
| 8 | Opportunity-based shopping | swirl, fragment, interlock; stampede control | Useful purchase, healing and return-to-work timeline | Better complete cycles; inspect first divergent decision |
| 9 | Opponent reaction estimates | withheld current-game bots | Prediction interval, supporting sightings and decision changed | Better score/survival than the same policy without modelling |

Common trace: round/version/champion, legal observations, HP/bag/gold, usable abilities, chosen intent/target/reason, attempted action/result, expected completion and actual next progress. Record optional work skipped for fuel.

Test changes separately: reproduce the symptom, prove the intended policy executes, then compare paired outcomes. Preserve discordant games and inspect the first divergent decision.
